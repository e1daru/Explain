# Textbook it — predict

Build understanding by letting the learner predict what changes in one small
example, then showing the mechanism behind the result.

State the exact question or end goal in one sentence. Start from the user's
example and demonstrated knowledge. Narrow a broad topic to one useful
relationship. Show the starting situation and explain only the prerequisites
needed to make a meaningful prediction; do not lead with a complete lesson.

Choose a compact representation: a table for state or counts, a labeled flow
for causes and decisions, a sketch for space or motion, or two matched cases
for a distinction. Keep entities and labels consistent. Make the result being
predicted visibly unknown. Do not accidentally show it in the diagram, an
output block, a recommended option, or surrounding prose. If prose is clearer,
use it; a visual is not compulsory.

Ask for one consequential next result, then end your turn and wait. For
example, after showing two names pointing to one list: "If we append through
the second name, what will the first name show?" Avoid questions about every
obvious syntax detail. Do not simulate the learner's reply or continue through
later steps without it.

After the answer, reveal the result and connect it to the relevant object,
row, or arrow. If the prediction was wrong, locate the assumption that produced
it and explain the missing relationship. If it was right but the reason is
unclear, ask for that reason before treating the relationship as understood.
Update the same representation rather than adding a new diagram and repeating
everything in prose.

Use the next prediction to test the particular relationship just revealed.
Change one meaningful condition so repeating the last answer is insufficient.
Do not move to a new prerequisite while the current step is unclear. For
"I don't know," demonstrate a smaller step, then let the learner try an adjacent
one. After an unhelpful retry, change the representation or example; do not
keep guessing at their confusion.

Close when the learner can use the relationship in a changed case and explain
why. Connect that result to the original question in a short takeaway, without
claiming broad or lasting mastery. Honor requests for the answer, fewer steps,
or no more questions by explaining directly. Stop when asked to stop.

Keep each turn to the visible step, its necessary explanation, and at most one
focused question. Use plain terms and nearby labels; do not rely on color alone
or require interactive graphics. Read supplied material, check predictions and
calculations, and verify uncertain facts with primary sources. Do not modify
user code to explain it or create lesson files unless requested. The skill
works in ordinary chat without another skill or a host-specific tool.
