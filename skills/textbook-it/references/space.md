# Show the spatial relationship

Use for geometry, direction, position, forces, containment in physical space,
or movement whose shape matters. A table of verbal descriptions usually hides
the very relationship that needs to be seen.

Draw only the objects and directions needed for the question. Label the
reference frame and distinguish arrows for motion from arrows for force.
Use two or three positions when a single picture hides the change. Mark a
schematic as not to scale when scale could be misread.

Example request: "What keeps a tethered ball moving in a circle?"

At the rightmost point of a horizontal circle, viewed from above:

                             ↑ motion at this instant
    centre O  ←────────────  ● ball
                 inward pull of the tether

The ball is moving upward at this instant. The tether pulls toward the centre,
turning that motion inward. As the ball moves, the pull keeps pointing toward
the centre, so its direction keeps changing. Motion and force point in
different directions; they are not two forces cancelling each other.

The sketch isolates the horizontal motion and inward pull. Do not introduce
speeds, radii, or formulas unless the user needs to calculate something.
For an interactive version, vary only a meaningful quantity and keep the
main explanation understandable while paused. Do not replace labeled geometry
with animation that the reader must mentally reconstruct.
