# Make the relevant quantity visible

Use when confusion comes from percentages, conditional groups, different
denominators, weights, units, or a changing quantity.

Name the target group before calculating. Use counts if they make the groups
easier to follow; label constructed counts as a model. Keep counts distinct
from rates. For averages, expose the weights. For time trends or relationships
between quantities, a simple labeled plot may work better than a large table.
Do not force all numerical problems into the same visual.

Example request: "What share of marked packages are fragile?"

Suppose a model batch has 1,000 packages: 100 fragile and 900 sturdy. A scanner
marks 90% of fragile packages and 10% of sturdy ones.

| Starting group | Packages | Marked |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fragile | 100 | 90 |
| Sturdy | 900 | 90 |
| Total | 1,000 | 180 |

We are choosing from the **180 marked packages**, so that is the denominator.
Of those, 90 are fragile: 90/180 = 50%. The scanner's 90% detection rate starts
with fragile packages; the question starts with marked packages.

The explanation of the denominator is the causal bridge here. A formula alone
does not provide it. Avoid extra precision, unlabeled units, and averaging
percentages from unequal groups as though the groups had equal weight.
